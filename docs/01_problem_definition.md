# 01 – Problem Definition

## 1. Situation
A manufacturer produces metal nuts on an automated line. At the end of the line,
workers inspect parts by eye. They get tired, inspection quality varies between shifts,
and defective parts occasionally reach customers, causing complaints, returns and
sometimes a recall of a whole batch.

## 2. Business problem
Visual inspection is slow, inconsistent and does not scale: defective parts slip
through, and the line cannot run faster than the inspectors.

## 3. Stakeholders
- **Quality manager:** wants no defective part to reach a customer, and evidence
  (images, logs) for every decision in case of a complaint or audit.
- **Production manager:** wants inspection that keeps up with line speed and does not
  reject so many good parts that throughput and scrap costs suffer.
- **Line operator:** needs a clear PASS / REJECT signal and, for rejected parts, a picture
  showing *where* the problem is, so a quick manual check is possible.

## 4. Goal and success criteria
**Goal:** automatically flag defective parts from a camera image, and show where the defect is.

**Which mistake is more expensive?**
- A **missed defect** reaches the customer: complaint, return, reputation damage.
- A **false reject** sends a good part to a short manual re-check.

We assume a missed defect costs about **20 times** a false reject (to be validated with
quality and finance). The system must **catch nearly all defects**, while keeping false
rejects low enough that the manual review station is not overloaded.

**Success criteria (initial targets):**
- Catch at least **95%** of defective parts.
- Wrongly reject at most **10%** of good parts.
- Show a heatmap of the suspected defect for every rejected part.
- Decide within the line's cycle time (target: well under 1 second per part).

## 5. Available data
**MVTec AD** (MVTec Software GmbH, Munich), the industry benchmark for visual anomaly
detection: high-resolution images of industrial products with real defects and pixel-exact
defect masks. We use the **metal_nut** category (other categories work with one setting).

Key property: the **training set contains only defect-free parts**. That is realistic:
factories have thousands of good parts but few, varied, unpredictable defects.

License: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, **non-commercial use only**. Fine for this portfolio project;
a commercial product would need its own images.

## 6. Technical requirements
- **FR1:** Anomaly score per part and a PASS / REJECT decision.
- **FR2:** Anomaly heatmap showing where the defect is.
- **FR3:** Operator review screen to confirm or overrule decisions, with a log.
- **FR4:** Export for edge hardware and measured latency.
- **NFR1:** Recall-first threshold chosen by business cost, adjustable.
- **NFR2:** Reproducible: fixed seeds, one command, results generated by code.

## 7. Constraints and risks
- **No defect examples for training:** supervised classification is not an option (see doc 02).
- **Unknown future defects:** the system must also catch defect types never seen before.
- **Lighting and camera changes** shift image statistics; the model must be re-fitted if the
  camera setup changes.
- **Logical defects** (a correct part placed upside down) look locally normal and are harder
  to detect than scratches.
- **Benchmark vs. reality:** MVTec images are taken under controlled conditions; a real line
  needs validation with its own camera.

## 8. Out of scope
- Mechanical part handling (rejecting the part physically).
- Measuring dimensions (metrology).
- Classifying the defect type automatically (possible extension).
